How quantum errors are handled, from noise to correction

One map of every method in IBM's six-episode series Quantum Computing Is Here: Tools for Quantum Advantage. Start with the pipeline to see where each method acts, then open the tree to see how it works. Each method is tagged by what it does to an error: detect it, correct it, or neither.

4. Run

Noise acts on the qubits

5. Check

Ancillas measure parities; syndrome comes out

inside circuit, detect

6. Decode and recover

Classical decoder reads the syndrome and applies a fix in the same shot
  • Lookup / majority vote
  • Matching, BP
live, each round, correct

7. Post-process

Fix the averages, or throw away bad shots
  • M3, T-Rex
  • ZNE, PEC
  • Post-selection
after measurement

Two things only error correction (stages 5 and 6) does: it detects and fixes an error within a single shot, and it keeps the quantum state alive for further computation. Suppression never looks at the error. Mitigation never sees an individual error, only the averaged damage. Detection sees the error, but its only "fix" is to discard the shot.

The tree

Click a node with ▸ to open it. Each description says what the method does and what it costs.

  • Ep 1Noise is the physical disturbance (thermal, crosstalk, imperfect pulses); an error is the wrong step it causes. Unlike a classical bit, a qubit can drift by any continuous amount in amplitude and phase. Noise is modelled by quantum channels: amplitude damping (T1), dephasing (T2), and the Pauli channel (X, Y, Z with probabilities). Everything below is a way to live with it.
    • context
      • SPAM errors are handled separately from errors inside the circuit; the two toolsets do not interfere.
      • Coherent = an unwanted unitary (over-rotation), adds up fast. Incoherent = a non-unitary channel, adds in quadrature. A recurring theme: convert coherent into incoherent.
      • T1 on IBM hardware is a few hundred µs, while two-qubit gates take from tens of nanoseconds up to about 100 ns, so idle time matters more than gate count for relaxation. Pauli channels can be created deliberately, which later methods exploit.
    • Ep 2NEITHERChange the circuit so errors occur less, differently, or accumulate less. Happens while the circuit runs, by inserting extra single-qubit gates. No error is ever detected or corrected.
      • NEITHERInsert X (or XY) pulses during idle time so unknown phase drift in the first half is undone in the second half. Works like a frequency filter on the noise. Best for long idle periods; for short idles it can lower fidelity because the inserted gates have their own error. Example: fidelity after 100 two-qubit-gate durations, 0.58 without DD, 0.83 with.
      • NEITHERWrap each two-qubit gate in random Paulis chosen so the logical effect is unchanged. On average this turns any noise channel into a Pauli channel: errors become classical randomness over X, Y, Z instead of coherent build-up. Often called error shaping rather than suppression, and it is what makes ZNE and PEC predictable.
    • Ep 3NEITHERAccept that the state is noisy; build a better estimator of the ideal expectation value from many noisy shots. Happens after measurement, in classical post-processing plus extra circuit variants. No individual error is seen or fixed, and the quantum state is not improved.
      • Targets the measurement step only.
        • NEITHERLearn how readouts are misassigned, but only on the subspace of bitstrings actually observed (31 × 31 instead of 2¹⁰ × 2¹⁰ in the GHZ example), then invert. Raw 66% of shots in the ideal states became 83%. Works with the sampler; lightweight calibration.
        • NEITHERRandomise the measurement basis with Paulis before readout, undo classically, then rescale with calibration circuits. Removes the asymmetric bias (1→0 more often than 0→1). Default resilience level 1 in Qiskit Runtime's estimator. Cannot be stacked naively with M3.
      • Targets errors inside the circuit.
        • NEITHERRun the circuit at noise levels 1, 3, 5 (gate folding G G† G, or probabilistic error amplification) and extrapolate to zero. Cost ≈ k × shots. Assumes the observable depends smoothly on noise. GHZ example: 17% error → 4% by hand, 0.987 with Runtime's variance-aware fit (resilience level 2). Combine with twirling and readout mitigation; not with PEC.
        • NEITHERLearn the noise channel per layer, build its inverse as a quasi-probability mix of physical operations, sample it and weight results by sign. Unbiased but shots scale like γ², exponential in depth. Most accurate in the series (0.667 vs ideal 0.665) but needs an accurate noise model and shallow circuits. Not combinable with ZNE.
      • Move the problem into the observable instead of the circuit.
        • NEITHERAbsorb the last circuit layers into the measured observable: shorter circuit, more complex observable, more sampling.
        • NEITHERPropagate a learned noise model into the observable so that measuring the modified observable on noisy hardware mitigates gate noise.
    • Ep 4DETECTFind out that an error happened. The response is post-selection: discard the shot. Key quantities: fidelity, post-selection rate, sampling overhead, detection region. A code with distance d detects any Pauli error of weight ≤ d − 1.
      • DETECT at final readoutfix = discardIf the ideal output must obey a rule (even parity, or all bits equal for a GHZ state), reject outputs that break it. 10-qubit GHZ: raw 60% → 85% with parity → 100% with all-agree, at the price of fewer kept shots. Limits: the state is already collapsed, Z errors are invisible, and you must know the ideal output.
      • DETECT mid-circuitfix = discardCopy parity (e.g. ZZ, or ZZZZ / XXXX for the 4-qubit logical Bell states) onto an ancilla with CNOTs and measure only the ancilla. Outcome −1 flags an error without collapsing the data. The ancilla outcomes are the syndrome. Misses errors above the distance and gives probabilistic answers for non-Pauli errors such as T1 decay.
      • DETECT around a payloadfix = discardCheck a Pauli R after a circuit region U and L = U†RU before it; the two must agree. Found by operator back-propagation through U. Needs no knowledge of the state, only of U. Detects any error in the region that anticommutes with the check.
      • DETECT + locatefix = discardPush L back to the all-zero start (stabilised by all-Z) and propagate forward classically. Catches errors in the first layers and tells you which qubit and which layer. Only a head start: propagating through the whole circuit classically would make the quantum computer unnecessary.
    • Ep 5DETECTCORRECTThe harder question: not "did something go wrong" but "what most likely went wrong". Four steps in every code: encode (spread one logical qubit over many physical with CNOTs) → syndrome (ancilla parity checks, the DETECT step) → decode (classical inference) → recover (apply the fix and keep computing, the CORRECT step). Needs d ≥ 2t + 1 to correct t errors, roughly twice the redundancy of detection. Below a threshold error rate, bigger codes help; above it they hurt.
      • DETECT: 2 parity ancillasCORRECT: majority voteEncode |ψ⟩ → α|000⟩+β|111⟩. Ancilla 0 checks q0⊕q1, ancilla 1 checks q1⊕q2; the pattern (10, 11, 01) names the flipped qubit. Logical error 3p² − 2p³, so it helps only for p < 1/2. Bit flips only; the teaching example for the four steps.
        • Syndrome → qubit to flip. Fails when two or more qubits flip.
      • DETECT: weight-4 X and Z checksCORRECT: close the chainData qubits on a square lattice, ancillas on faces (Z checks) and vertices (X checks), nearest-neighbour gates only, no SWAPs. A single error fires two adjacent checks; a chain of errors fires only its endpoints, and the decoder connects them by the most likely (shortest) path. Any closed loop is a product of stabilizers, so a wrong-but-equivalent chain still corrects. Threshold ≈ 1%; logical error ∝ (p/p_th)^((d+1)/2); cost ≈ (2d+1)² qubits per logical qubit, about 1000 for d ≈ 21 at p = 10⁻³. Bravyi–Poulin–Terhal bound limits all 2D local codes this way.
        • A row of X's commutes with every check and is a logical X; a column of Z's is a logical Z. A line of errors across the grid is therefore an undetectable logical error; its length is the distance d.
        • Pair up fired checks by shortest paths; the standard choice because short chains are more probable than long ones.
      • DETECT: low-weight checksCORRECT: BP-type decodersKeep few-qubit parity checks but drop the planar nearest-neighbour rule. Finite rate: logical qubits grow in proportion to physical qubits, which the surface code cannot do. Price: long-range connectivity and harder decoding.
        • Surface code rolled into a torus: no boundaries, translation-invariant. Loops that wrap the torus are logical operators (or logical errors). A connectivity model, not a physical donut.
        • IBM's code; checks distributed by a Tanner graph with long-range links. 144 data qubits store 12 logical qubits at distance 12, about an order of magnitude better than the surface code on k·d²/n. Trade-offs: decoding complexity and the hardware for non-local checks.
      • enables computationStabilizer codes handle Clifford gates natively, but Cliffords alone are classically simulable. A T gate needs a magic state prepared outside the code, injected with CNOTs + measurement + conditional correction, and first purified by magic state distillation (a "factory"), because raw magic states are far noisier than the 10⁻¹² target.
    • Ep 6workflowThe directed execution model: label circuit layers, learn noise once per unique layer, apply different strategies to different regions.
      • Boxes the circuit, lets you declare twirling / noise injection per box, generates circuit variants and post-processing instructions. The "recipe".
      • Runs template + samplex and returns samples. The "chef".
      • Learns Pauli–Lindblad models per boxed layer (and T-Rex for measurements); the learned model can be injected back for PEA/PEC.
      • Maps where in space-time an X, Y or Z error would actually affect a low-weight observable, so expensive mitigation (PEC) is spent only where it matters.
      • Build and shorten circuits before any error handling: replace early layers with a shallower approximation, better Trotter formulas, split big circuits, map chemistry and optimisation problems to qubits.
